Math Teaching

Math teachers help people make sense of ideas and find ways through confusion.

Your student says: “I know 3 × 4 is 12, but I don’t understand why.” Help them see what multiplication means.
  1. Choose a model: equal groups, an array, or a number line.
  2. Sketch it or describe it in one or two sentences.
  3. Ask a question that would help you check the student’s understanding.

Pure Math

Pure mathematicians explore patterns, structures, and why mathematical statements are true.

Explore: Look at the first few square numbers: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25… Compare each one with the square before it.
  1. Find the differences between neighboring squares.
  2. Predict the next difference and the next square number.
  3. Try to explain why the differences follow that pattern.

Use math to solve a real problem05:00

Applied Math

Applied mathematicians build and use mathematical models to help answer practical questions.

Design challenge: A short ramp rises 3 feet over a horizontal run of 12 feet. The slope is rise ÷ run. Imagine making the ramp twice as long while keeping the same steepness.
  1. Calculate the original slope.
  2. Double the horizontal run. What rise keeps the slope the same?
  3. What real-world details might a simple slope calculation leave out?

Statistics & Data Science

Statisticians and data scientists look for useful patterns while checking how data was collected and what it can tell us.

Question: How long, in minutes, did seven students take to get to school? Data: 8, 10, 10, 12, 13, 14, 45
  1. Find the mean (average) and the median (middle value).
  2. Which better describes a “typical” trip here? Why?
  3. What would you want to know before drawing a conclusion about all students?
